## Observed qualities and transferable principles

### Resintoi

The desktop opening places a large wordmark and a shorter proposition inside a dark teal rounded surface, with a substantial portrait to the right. Later samples pair daylight portraits, foliage and architecture; an impact section contrasts a wide landscape image with a narrower light content panel. Scale changes between the brand, proposition, explanation and compact metadata are visible. On mobile, proposition, wordmark and portrait stack inside the same visual language.

Study the relationship between brand scale, media framing and readable explanation, and the coherent light/color of the photography. Do not turn its plus signs, metadata, blurred plaques or oversized wordmark into defaults. Its sampled hero dimensions are not a replacement for our content-safe viewport policy.

### Sparkles

The desktop hero balances a navy text panel against cleaning-action video, with cream, pale blue and lavender carried into controls and service components. Condensed headings contrast with ordinary readable supporting text. Service cards show the work and tools; the mobile hero brings video above the proposition and retains booking/pricing actions. The opened mobile menu uses the same typography and cream surface, with a service disclosure and a dimmed page behind it.

Study concrete service imagery, action hierarchy and consistent component treatment. Friendly does not require many badges. Ratings, availability messages and claims are template content, not reusable proof. Decorative arrow badges remain excluded under the user's rules.

### Algarve Studio

The opening combines oversized uppercase type with an animated repeated/sliced word treatment. Scrolling reveals a dense mosaic of portrait, fashion, product and nature imagery around a landscape video; the arrangement changes and the central media becomes more dominant. A startup percentage screen was observed, but its readiness logic was not tested.

Study how one expressive typographic treatment and a coordinated media sequence establish a recognizable studio voice. This is useful for an image-rich creative brief, not a requirement for every business. Coordinates, labels, repeated letters and complicated motion are not substitutes for content. Do not reproduce a percentage loader without real progress or assume this template meets our loading, accessibility or overflow criteria.

### Good Biz

Home A uses a large rounded photograph with text over its darker left portion, followed by a service composition with explanatory text beside a group of lightly rotated purple/white cards. Home B instead leads with a centered typographic proposition containing small image inserts. The rounded sans lettering, generous lead copy, purple accents and restrained controls hold the variants together.

Study approachable proportions, readable secondary copy and a coherent shape language. A centered headline or service grid can be appropriate when well composed. Do not copy the inline image capsules, card rotation or section order by habit. Placeholder paragraphs need replacement with specific content before judging the finished composition.

### Consult Corner

Home A uses a centered large headline, a substantial explanation and a single primary action. A curved lower edge introduces business photography; services use white rounded cards on a pale background, with headings, small icons and readable descriptions. Home B changes to a text/media split with a large blue-toned visual while retaining the lilac accent and business typography.

Study comprehensible grouping, repeated alignments and consistent spacing across information-heavy components. This reference demonstrates that familiar business compositions remain valid. Its paired slogan, colored ending, generic icons or adjacent arrow are not instructions to revive those as automatic recipes. Preserve clarity while writing content and choosing media for the actual brief.

### Positive Studio

Home A uses strongly condensed black display type, broad readable supporting copy and a green graphic insert on a warm pale background. Later samples change between dark and light rounded section surfaces and use large client-name bands and offset text/statistics. Home B's opening adds a layered composition of bright cards, portrait and device imagery rather than simply recoloring Home A.

Study the contrast between expressive display type and generous reading text, and how a limited set of surfaces connects different sections. Heavy type, oversized client bands, inline graphics and large gaps are options, not a universal premium style. Sampled scroll reveals included intermediate states; do not copy hidden-content bands or infer reliable animation behavior from them.

### UBI

Home A combines a large serif proposition with sans-serif explanation and controls inside a pale rounded panel. Yellow identifies primary actions and carries into a prominent dashboard illustration. Following samples use large numerical summaries and a phone-in-hand product presentation. Home B brings that phone image into a yellow hero beside the proposition and statistics.

Study how the product itself supplies visual evidence and how one accent connects the marketing page and represented interface. Serif typography can work beyond editorial or hospitality sites when its proportions fit. The displayed financial claims and template statistics are not verified results and must not be reused as facts. A product illustration is not proof of working software.

### Lumes

The preview is branded “Lume.” Its opening gives a vivid blue/red portrait most of the screen, with white condensed typography near the lower edge and a narrow outer frame. Further scrolling shows a dark composition where images and a graphic object move in relation to large text. The mobile opening simplifies the title/media arrangement; its menu is a compact black dropdown, not an elaborate overlay.

Study the strength of a deliberately cropped image, limited typography and coordinated movement. Do not copy fashion imagery into unrelated sectors, the decorative object, coordinates, automatic eyebrow squares or the whole pinned sequence. The mobile menu and hero sizing are observations, not independently approved solutions to our navigation and viewport requirements.

### Plan Tool

Home A centers a large navy/blue proposition above a readable introduction and an email field directly paired with its action. Supporting benefit labels sit below, followed by blue section surfaces, chart imagery, a people photograph and an overlaid statistic panel. Home B instead pairs a left-aligned proposition and action with a large rounded photograph. The palette, input/button treatment and curves remain consistent across these arrangements.

Study the clear relationship between a proposition and its immediate action, and continuity between forms, imagery and supporting sections. The gradient rule, highlighted headline word, icons and floating statistics must not become defaults. Replace placeholder copy and invented percentages; assess any real form's validation and recovery separately from its appearance.

## Working interpretation of the selection

The selection spans friendly service websites, quiet business layouts, expressive studios and product marketing. It does not support forcing every brief into a sparse editorial serif, a giant condensed headline or an experimental composition.

Useful principles to test in the next render:

- **Visible hierarchy beyond headings:** lead copy, explanations, controls and details have deliberate proportions and line lengths. Meaningful copy remains readable.
- **A coherent identity across components:** type, spacing, image framing, surfaces and interaction belong together; originality is not a requirement to restyle every button.
- **Media that does a job:** show the service, work or product. Select and crop imagery as part of composition rather than filling an empty rectangle with industry stock imagery.
- **Rhythm appropriate to the content:** choose where to concentrate information and where to give it room. Conventional grids, centered openings and splits are available tools; repetition without a content reason is the problem.
- **Motion chosen as part of the experience:** where justified, coordinate a few meaningful relationships. Do not animate everything or let content remain invisible while waiting for effects.

These are inferred principles to validate against actual output. A named inspiration and a list of prohibited motifs are insufficient. In the early rendered review, identify where the chosen principles appear and where execution still falls short; correct those concrete issues before extending the design.

## Boundaries and observation coverage

Explicit user constraints still govern: no decorative arrows unless requested, no automatic eyebrow adornments, a content-safe viewport-based homepage hero, the required preloader and real route transitions, and the prescribed SPA/source/build architecture. A liked template can contain treatments that conflict with these rules. Take relevant qualities without importing those treatments. Do not copy brands, licensed assets or template code, fabricate testimonials, adopt Webflow as the implementation stack, or claim that consulting references trains model weights.

Desktop inspection used a 1280 × 800 viewport: opening and selected supporting sections for all nine references. The additional Home B checks covered opening compositions for Good Biz, Consult Corner, Positive Studio, UBI and Plan Tool. Algarve and Lumes coverage concentrated on their opening/media sequences; it did not establish the quality of every later section or project page. No claim is made about unvisited Home C variants or complete template libraries.

Mobile inspection at 390 × 844 covered opening layouts for Resintoi, Sparkles and Lumes. Sparkles and Lumes menus were opened and dismissed with Escape; focus trapping, focus restoration and all submenu behavior were not audited. Mobile adaptation of the other six references remains unverified here.

This is visual calibration, not a template certification. Observed entry/scroll changes do not establish timing, actual loading progress, FOUC prevention, route transitions, reduced-motion support, accessibility compliance or performance. No complete forms, purchase flows or backend behavior were tested. Inspect relevant live states anew when using a reference for a specific interaction decision.
