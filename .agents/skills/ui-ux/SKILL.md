# UI / UX Design — Premium Digital Quality

## Objective
Deliver distinctive, considered interfaces that make complex products understandable and pleasant to use. Beautiful must also mean legible, accessible, stable and effective.

## Process
1. Learn product goals, audience, key flows, actual content, brand constraints and any established design system.
2. For new designs or substantial redesigns, inspect a small selection of relevant live references, using Awwwards for discovery and direct sites outside award galleries. For demos, also consult [BYQ templates](https://app.byq.supply/templates) and inspect a relevant template's live preview when available. Study desktop and mobile composition, typography, components, navigation/menu behavior, entry/loading and page transitions where observable. Record exact reference/preview URLs and concrete observations in the existing brief or handoff; distinguish a catalog thumbnail from a rendered site or tested interaction. If no suitable preview is accessible, state that limitation and continue with available evidence. For targeted adjustments, reuse the established direction and references unless a specific gap requires more research. Do not claim visits, motion checks or model training that did not occur.
3. Choose an art direction for this brief: composition, type roles, imagery, color and motion. Start from actual content without habitual ornaments; extract useful principles from references without copying their branding or layout. BYQ and other templates are design references, not authorization to import their code, assets, dependencies or platform. For a demo collection, compare the preceding demos and give each new brand a distinct direction; do not change fonts or colors at random just to appear different.
4. Map information architecture and real navigation before composing screens. A single long page is a deliberate choice, not the default way to avoid designing routes and transitions.
5. Inventory the components actually needed and define their typography, spacing and relevant states, including first entry, loading, empty, validation, error, success, disabled and interrupted interaction. For new websites and demos, include the default required startup preloader and transitions for actual routes unless the user explicitly opts out. Do not add unrelated components just to satisfy a checklist.
6. Inspect the rendered result on mobile and desktop, with long content, keyboard operation and reduced motion. Refine alignment, reading rhythm, contrast, interaction and perceived loading, then fix visible defects within scope.

## Standards
- Compose around the content and the user's next action. Do not reuse the same centered hero, alternating splits, card grid and closing CTA for every brief, or replace that recipe with arbitrary asymmetry, giant type or empty space.
- Do not fill margins with tiny labels, status chips or slogans. Each text fragment must help orientation, explain content or communicate an action.
- Establish readable roles for lead copy, paragraphs, supporting text, captions, labels and controls, using size, weight, line-height and measure deliberately. Do not make all non-heading text look alike or shrink meaningful information into microtext. Choose heading and text families for the brand and language; neither an editorial serif nor TikTok Sans is a universal default.
- Motion must reinforce understanding. Use GSAP for complex orchestration where justified; integrate `gsap-react`, `gsap-performance` and reduced-motion considerations.
- Preserve accessibility; hover-only and mouse-only UI is incomplete.
- Reuse existing components and tokens unless there is a clear reason to redesign.
- Never generate a decorative showcase image just to announce task completion.

## Deliberate ornament and iconography
- Do not add decorative arrows to buttons, text links, clickable cards or scroll prompts unless explicitly requested by the user. This covers inline/trailing arrows, adjacent badges, hover/focus reveals, Unicode glyphs, SVG/icon libraries and CSS-generated shapes. A navigation destination, external URL or booking action does not make an ornamental arrow functional.
- Retain directional controls when direction or disclosure is the actual interaction, such as gallery previous/next controls and select/disclosure indicators. Do not remove meaningful controls or unrelated icons merely because decorative arrows are excluded.
- Use an eyebrow only when its text supplies useful context. Plain text is the starting point; a line, dot, bullet, square, sparkle, icon, capsule or badge is not part of the default eyebrow component. A selective accent may support a specific brand or hierarchy decision. Compare with the undecorated version and keep it only when it adds something concrete; it must not become a compulsory motif on every section.
- Before handoff, review the full composition for habitual formulas. Removing an arrow or replacing a dash with a dot is insufficient if the same generic layout and ornament recipe remains. Rework the underlying choice from the brief, content and references; do not substitute another universal style.

## Homepage hero sizing
- Unless the user specifies otherwise, compose the homepage hero around the available viewport width and height. With content that fits comfortably, the header, hero and intended outer spacing should fill the opening screen. Avoid fixed pixel heights or maximum-height caps that make it too tall on short screens and too short on tall screens.
- Use content-safe minimum sizing rather than clipping or shrinking copy to force a one-screen layout. Adapt typography, spacing and media crops to width and height; allow natural vertical growth for long content, short landscape screens or zoom. A viewport-sized hero does not prescribe centered text, a particular image treatment or one reusable layout.
- Coordinate header placement, mobile browser chrome, safe areas and overflow handling with `css-scss`. Check both short and tall viewports; all essential content and controls must remain reachable.

## Whole-experience design
- Carry the chosen direction through composition, typography, imagery, links/CTAs, cards/lists, forms, filters, menus, overlays and footer wherever they exist. A strong hero cannot compensate for an unfinished rest of the experience.
- Give each component a clear content role and behavior. Repeated components should share a coherent language; hierarchy and purpose, not a requirement for every button to look unique, determine variation.
- Design the applicable default, hover, focus, pressed/selected, disabled, pending, empty, error and success states. For forms and filters, cover validation, feedback and recovery; for overlays, cover dismissal, focus and return to context. Keep decorative treatments subordinate to comprehension and use.
- Review complete journeys and the full page, including secondary routes and the footer. Recheck component consistency, content density, imagery crops, reading order and interaction feedback at mobile and desktop sizes.

## Navigation and motion
- Design the navigation with the same care as the page. Choose visible links, a drawer or a composed overlay based on content and available space; a hamburger is not an automatic desktop design. Specify open/close choreography, item hierarchy, active destination, hover/focus and touch states.
- Menus must handle Escape, focus placement and restoration, and scroll-lock cleanup. Trap focus only for modal menus and keep the background inert while modal. Use meaningful link labels and `aria-current` for the active destination; closing or interrupted animation must not leave invisible links focusable.
- Implement first entry and real page transitions as one designed sequence. Transitions are required by default for new websites/demos and loading/navigation redesigns with internal routes unless the user opts out; reduced motion provides immediate navigation. Unrelated fixes on an existing site do not authorize a navigation redesign. Specify the outgoing view, pending destination, prepared incoming view and interruption/error behavior before implementation. Anchor scrolling and hover effects do not count as page transitions; do not invent routes solely to demonstrate motion.
- For new React sites, coordinate route changes within a persistent shell. Retain the outgoing view or a stable transition surface until the destination's critical presentation is ready, then reveal it without a blank or partially styled intermediate frame. For an existing or explicitly selected multi-document site, choose supported cross-document transitions and stable initial loading with a usable fallback. An exit fade, `location.assign()` after a timeout, and an independent entry animation do not establish destination readiness or seamless navigation.
- Unless the user specifies another treatment, the default is a complete fade-out of the outgoing page view, an immediate switch at exit completion, then an immediate fade-in of the prepared destination. Keep the sequence short and continuous; do not leave a partly faded page waiting, pause on a blank screen, or overlap unfinished exit and entry fades. A deliberately persistent application shell may remain visible.
- Prepare only the destination resources needed for a usable first view while the outgoing view remains fully visible. Begin the exit when the destination can be committed; if loading is slow or fails, provide useful pending/recovery feedback on the readable outgoing view. Chain the switch and entry to animation completion and the prepared render, without an extra timer or artificial hold. Clean up cancelled transitions and preserve immediate reduced-motion navigation.
- Preserve deep links, browser Back/Forward, expected scroll restoration and focus after navigation. Handle rapid repeated navigation and interrupted animations; reduced motion must allow immediate usable navigation without waiting for choreography.

## First paint and loading
- For new websites/demos and loading/navigation redesigns, design and implement a startup preloader unless the user explicitly opts out. It belongs to the initial HTML/loading shell, with enough early CSS to render coherently before application initialization; it cannot depend on React mounting, a delayed effect or GSAP downloading. Define its visual treatment, actual readiness conditions and handoff to the first usable view.
- Specify the first visible content frame: correct background, critical styles, stable media dimensions, readable font fallbacks and prepared animation start states. Fix CSS FOUC, font swaps, layout shifts and reload/animation flashes at their respective sources with ENGINEER. Do not use a covering animation as the sole repair.
- Tie release to the first usable render and a small explicit set of critical resources. Reveal as soon as they are ready or their usable fallback is selected. Do not invent progress, force a minimum delay or wait for all fonts, every image, a video ending, or below-the-fold assets. A cached entry may finish immediately; still demonstrate that the loader works under a slow first load.
- Arm timeout/error recovery independently of the application bundle before loading can block access. If scripts or assets fail, show available content or a clear recovery state; a callback in the failed bundle is not a fallback. Avoid permanent hidden documents, trapped focus and retained scroll locks. Respect reduced motion and avoid announcing every animation frame to assistive technology.
- Provide a baseline HTML/CSS recovery state if JavaScript is disabled or the early bootstrap/watchdog is blocked. A permanently covering static loader is unacceptable. Expose meaningful explanation/recovery without requiring the failed script; a complete app without JavaScript is not required. Verify both disabled JavaScript and blocked bootstrap, not only a failed application bundle.

## Acceptance checks
- Inspect the full page at desktop and mobile sizes: purposeful section rhythm, readable supporting text, distinct typographic roles, and no repeated decorative device without a content reason.
- Inspect buttons, links, cards and scroll prompts in default, hover and focus states for decorative arrows, even isolated ones. Check eyebrows for automatic markers or cosmetic substitutions; retain only intentional accents permitted by the project direction.
- Resize the homepage between tall and short desktop and mobile/landscape viewports. When content fits, confirm the opening composition fills the available screen; with zoom or longer content, confirm natural growth without clipped text, hidden controls or horizontal overflow.
- Observe cold and warm entry, slow or failed critical assets, and reduced motion. Capture the initial loading state, readiness handoff and first visible content through a recording, filmstrip or equivalent timed browser evidence. Exercise timeout, application-initialization failure, disabled JavaScript and blocked bootstrap with a meaningful recovery state. A settled screenshot or source inspection cannot prove absence of a flash.
- Exercise the menu with mouse, touch and keyboard; verify opening, closing, active links, focus restoration and interrupted states.
- Exercise actual route links, direct entry, refresh, rapid navigation and Back/Forward, including a delayed/failed destination and reduced motion. Observe outgoing, pending and incoming states rather than merely checking the final URL. Report startup/preloader, first-paint stability and route transitions separately as pass/fail/not-tested, with evidence; use not-applicable for route transitions only when there are no real internal routes or the user opted out. Do not report the experience complete while a required criterion fails or remains unverified.

## Handoff
Provide the concise design rationale and reference observations, type roles, navigation/loading decisions, checked interactions and unresolved constraints. Keep this in the normal brief or handoff rather than adding a separate design-process file for every page.
